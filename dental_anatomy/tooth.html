Root Surfaces

Labial / Buccal Root Surface
Convex, smooth. May show longitudinal grooves in some teeth.
Lingual / Palatal Root Surface
Convex, narrower than labial. Tapers toward the apex.
Mesial Root Surface
Often shows a developmental depression (root concavity).
Distal Root Surface
Convex, usually smoother than mesial surface.
07
◆ SURFACE TOPOGRAPHY

Elevations & Depressions

⬆️

Elevations

Cingulum — Bulge on the cervical third of the lingual surface.
Marginal Ridge — Linear elevation along the mesial/distal edges.
Cusp Ridge — Elevation from cusp tip descending along the surface.
Incisal Ridge — Rounded ridge at the incisal edge after wear.
Cervical Ridge — Rounded elevation on the cervical third of the crown.
⬇️

Depressions

Lingual Fossa — Shallow depression on the lingual surface.
Developmental Groove — Sharp line marking the fusion of lobes.
Supplemental Groove — Secondary shallow groove, non-lobal.
Pit — Small pinpoint depression, often at groove junctions.
Root Depression — Longitudinal concavity on the root.
08
◆ CLINICAL RELEVANCE

Clinical Notes

💉

Endodontic Considerations

Standard root canal treatment protocols apply. Access cavity design must respect the pulp chamber morphology.

🔧

Restorative Considerations

Cavity preparation should preserve marginal ridges. Match anatomy of the neighboring surfaces for optimal occlusion.

🩹

Extraction Considerations

Elevator and forcep application dictated by root morphology. Consider root curvature and adjacent structures.
