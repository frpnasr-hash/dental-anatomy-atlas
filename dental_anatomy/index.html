◆ FUNDAMENTALS

Tooth Anatomy 101

Every tooth is a marvel of biological engineering. Explore the fundamental structures shared by all teeth.

🔷

Enamel

The hardest substance in the human body (96% mineral). Translucent outer covering of the crown that protects underlying dentin.

🔶

Dentin

Bulk of the tooth. A yellowish tissue harder than bone but softer than enamel. Contains microscopic tubules.

💠

Pulp

The vital core — a soft tissue containing blood vessels, nerves, and connective tissue. Housed inside the pulp chamber and root canals.

◆

Cementum

Bone-like calcified tissue covering the root surface. Anchors the periodontal ligament fibers to the tooth.

🔺

Periodontal Ligament

A network of collagen fibers connecting the tooth root (via cementum) to the alveolar bone socket.

🔻

Alveolar Bone

The ridge of bone in the maxilla and mandible that contains the tooth sockets and supports the teeth.

Numbering Systems

Three internationally recognized systems used to identify every tooth in the mouth.

01

Universal Numbering

1 – 32

Used mainly in the United States. Numbering begins at the maxillary right third molar (#1), sweeps across to the maxillary left third molar (#16), drops to the mandibular left third molar (#17), and ends at the mandibular right third molar (#32).

🇺🇸 ADA Standard
02

FDI (ISO 3950)

11 – 48

Two-digit system used worldwide. First digit = quadrant (1: max-right, 2: max-left, 3: mand-left, 4: mand-right). Second digit = tooth position (1: central incisor → 8: third molar).

🌍 International
03

Palmer Notation

┘1 – ┐8

A graphical system that uses a right-angle symbol to indicate the quadrant, and a number (1–8) to indicate the tooth. Commonly used in orthodontics and oral surgery.
